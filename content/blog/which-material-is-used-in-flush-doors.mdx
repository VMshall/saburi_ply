<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What is a Flush Door?</strong></h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A flush door has a flat, plain surface without any panelling or moulding. It is usually made from a solid or hollow core covered on both sides with a smooth veneer or plywood. The result is a clean, modern-looking door that is easy to maintain and fits equally well in homes, offices, hospitals, and hotels.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Materials Inside a Flush Door</strong></h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Not all flush doors are made the same way. The core material determines strength, weight, sound insulation, and durability.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Solid core flush doors are made of hard timber with battens usually not less than 25mm wide, evenly distributed, with plywood sheets glued together to form the door shutter.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Hollow core flush doors use a wooden or composite frame with a honeycomb cardboard or plastic interior, covered with thin panels of MDF, plywood, or high-density fibreboard to give a smooth, flat finish.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The face material on the exterior can be plywood, laminated board, or veneer. After the core is dried and seasoned, it is glued with waterproof adhesive and pressed at high pressure, then calibrated to ensure uniform thickness across both faces.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For areas with moisture, the bonding adhesive matters just as much as the core. Under IS:2202, flush door shutters are categorised into two grades: BWP (Boiling Water Proof) grade and MR (Moisture Resistance) grade. Choose BWP grade for bathrooms, kitchens, and ground-floor rooms. MR grade works well for dry interior spaces.</p>
